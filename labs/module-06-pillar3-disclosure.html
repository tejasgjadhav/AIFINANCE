A Pillar 3 disclosure from one sentence

We take a bank's capital and leverage numbers, compute the six ratios a regulator reads first, and write the Basel III Pillar 3 document in Word. Then we do the part that matters. We check our document against the ratios the bank itself published.

A  build the document  →  B  check it against the bank's own numbers  →  C  peer review and AI commentary
Everything here is free, and no key is needed. Codex writes the code. Python and python-docx build the Word file. The commentary at the end can be written by pasting the ratio tables into any free AI chat. There is nothing to buy for this lab.
The data is real and it is a bank's. The capital and leverage inputs are ICICI Bank's own figures from its Basel III Pillar 3 disclosure at 31 March 2025, consolidated, read from pages 11, 12, 75 and 76 of the published PDF. The LCR and NSFR inputs are illustrative teaching values, because the bank publishes those two ratios in its annual report and quarterly liquidity disclosure, not in the Pillar 3 document. The data file says which is which.

ABuild the document30 minutes

We type one sentence. Codex asks what it needs, writes a plan, and waits for approval before it builds anything.

Here is a bank's capital, RWA and leverage data in bank_data.json. Compute CET1, Tier 1, total capital, leverage, LCR and NSFR, compare each with the RBI minimum, and write a Pillar 3 disclosure in Word with the DF-2 and DF-18 tables.

What the script actually does

  1. Reads the data. One JSON file with the capital stack, the capital requirement by risk type, total risk weighted assets, and the leverage exposures.
  2. Divides six times. CET1 over RWA. Tier 1 over RWA. Total capital over RWA. Tier 1 over total exposure. HQLA over 30-day outflows. Available over required stable funding.
  3. Builds the floor. For a D-SIB, the total capital minimum is 9.0% base plus the 2.5% conservation buffer plus the 0.2% surcharge, which is 11.7%. CET1 starts at 5.5% and Tier 1 at 7.0%, with the same buffers on top.
  4. Puts each ratio beside its floor. The gap between them is the headroom, in percentage points.
  5. Writes the Word file. Table DF-2 for capital adequacy, Table DF-18 for the leverage ratio, a liquidity table, and a commentary section, in the RBI layout.
  6. Reconciles. It prints the computed ratios next to the ratios the bank published, and says MATCH or MISMATCH on each line.

LCRHQLA / 30-day net outflowsCan the bank pay a month of stressed outflows from assets it can sell today?
NSFRAvailable / required stable fundingIs the balance sheet funded for a year, not just a month?
A mismatch is a stop. If your total capital ratio comes out at 16.9% and the bank published 16.41%, the document is not late. It is wrong. Find the input that was mis-typed or the formula that divided by the wrong thing. A Pillar 3 disclosure that does not reconcile to the bank's own numbers is never issued.

CPeer review and AI commentary15 minutes

  1. Swap documents with the next desk. Open theirs. Check the four ratios against the published ones without looking at their script.
  2. Paste the two ratio tables into any free AI chat. Ask for a one-paragraph management commentary on capital adequacy.
  3. Check every number in its paragraph against your tables. A wrong number, an invented trend, or a ratio it was never given is a strike.
  4. Keep only the sentences that survive. Paste them into the commentary section of the Word file. That is the AI-written part of the disclosure, and it has now been read by a person.
The rule for this hour. Automation earns its place by doing the division and the formatting. It never earns the right to skip the reconciliation, and no disclosure leaves without a human name on it.
