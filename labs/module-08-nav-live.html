Build a NAV report, reconcile it, explain it

We price a ten-holding equity fund, check its books against the custodian and the transfer agent, fix the breaks, and write the daily NAV commentary. The instructor first shows Claude's reconciliation skill doing the checking.

A  watch the skill  →  B  run the NAV engine  →  C  write the commentary
Free and keyless. The script uses built-in Python only. No API key, no installs, no internet. Run it in Codex, Google Colab or any Python 3.
The fund is made up. The holdings are named Stock A to Stock J and the prices are illustrative, not market data.

0The three files, and what each one saysread first

FileWho writes itWhat is in it
fund_books.csvThe fund accountantEach holding, the units the fund thinks it owns, and today's price. This gives market value.
custodian.csvThe custodian bankThe units the bank actually holds for each holding, and the cash in the fund's account.
fund_info.csvThe fund accountant, plus one line from the transfer agentCash per the books ₹2,60,00,000. Accrued expenses ₹8,50,000. Units outstanding per the books 1,00,00,000. Units outstanding per the transfer agent 99,80,000.

Accrued expenses are money the fund owes but has not paid yet, such as this month's fee to the fund manager. The NAV subtracts them today, before the cash leaves.

AWatch the skill20 minutes

The instructor opens the lab folder in Claude with the finance plugin and types this.

Use the reconciliation skill. Reconcile the fund's books (fund_books.csv and fund_info.csv) against the custodian statement (custodian.csv) and the transfer agent's units outstanding. List every break, say what most likely caused it, and propose the entry that fixes it. Then work out the NAV after the fixes.

What to watch for:

  • It lines up the records. Books against custodian for units and cash, books against the transfer agent for units outstanding.
  • It sorts each difference. A corporate action not booked, income not booked, a transaction booked on one side only.
  • It proposes the fix, and stops. A person approves every entry. The skill never posts anything.

BRun the NAV engine30 minutes

cd module-08-nav
python3 nav_engine.py

It prints four steps.

  1. NAV from the fund's books. Ten holdings at ₹43.37 crore, cash ₹2.60 crore, expenses ₹0.09 crore. Net assets ₹45.88 crore over 1 crore units gives a NAV of ₹45.8800.
  2. Reconciliation. Three breaks: Stock H shows 4,000 units in the books and 8,000 at the custodian, which is a 2-for-1 split not booked. The custodian holds ₹1,20,000 more cash, which is a dividend not booked. The transfer agent shows 20,000 fewer units, which is a redemption not booked.
  3. NAV after the fixes. ₹45.8920. The split changes nothing in value. The dividend adds cash. The redemption takes units out at NAV, so it does not move the NAV.
  4. Files and a prompt. It writes nav_report.csv and breaks.csv, which open in Excel or Google Sheets, and prints a prompt for the commentary.

CWrite the commentary30 minutes

  1. Paste the printed prompt into any free AI chat. It asks for a five-sentence daily NAV commentary that uses only the numbers given.
  2. Check every number in the answer against nav_report.csv. A number that is not in the file is a mistake, even if it sounds right.
  3. Swap reports with the next desk. Check their NAV and their breaks without looking at their screen.
