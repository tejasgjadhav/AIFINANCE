About the Course

Agentic AI & Advanced Analytics in Finance is a 30-hour specialization course for Semester III PGDM students at ISBMS, designed to bridge theoretical finance and contemporary AI-driven industry practice. The course equips students with production-grade skills in demand at Global Capability Centres (GCCs) and financial institutions across India and globally. Students explore agentic AI architectures, prompt engineering, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines, algorithmic trading, credit and market risk modelling (PD models, Monte Carlo VaR), regulatory compliance automation (Basel III/IV), and fund administration workflows. Drawing from Harvard Case Method, NUS sandbox-first learning, GARP's FRM framework, and CFA Institute standards, each module culminates in a deployable project artifact that builds a live portfolio. The capstone simulates a real GCC hiring panel, ensuring students are placement-ready upon completion.

Word count: ~130 words

Course Objectives (Bloom's Taxonomy)

  1. To recall key concepts of Large Language Models, AI agent architectures, and financial data APIs as applied in financial services operations. L1 – Remember
  2. To explain the principles of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), Model Context Protocol, and multi-agent orchestration within the context of financial workflow automation. L2 – Understand
  3. To apply prompt engineering, Python programming, and quantitative modelling techniques in hands-on lab sessions to solve real-world financial problems. L3 – Apply
  4. To analyse risk metrics (VaR, PD), regulatory compliance requirements (Basel III/IV), and algorithmic trading performance using AI-assisted analytical tools. L4 – Analyse
  5. To evaluate enterprise AI governance frameworks, model risk management policies, and the regulatory and ethical implications of deploying AI in financial institutions. L5 – Evaluate
  6. To design an integrated financial AI system—encompassing a fund administration module, risk analytics engine, and compliance report generator—demonstrating placement-ready skills for GCC and financial sector roles. L6 – Create

Assessments

Assignment: AI-Powered Monte Carlo VaR Risk Report

Introduced in: Session 5 (Hour 2)  |  Submitted to Academic Coordinator: At least 2 sessions before due date

Conducted: In-class lab component (Session 5, Hour 3) + individual take-home writeup


Description: Students will develop a Python-based Monte Carlo Value-at-Risk (VaR) model for a hypothetical equity portfolio of five NSE-listed stocks. The assignment requires students to: (1) collect 2 years of historical price data using Yahoo Finance API in Python (VS Code), (2) run 10,000 Monte Carlo simulation paths using NumPy, (3) calculate 95% and 99% VaR and CVaR, (4) generate an AI-written risk commentary using Claude API, and (5) interpret results with reference to Basel III market risk capital requirements.


Deliverables:

  • Python script (.py) with all code, charts, and simulation output (VS Code project)
  • 2-page PDF summary report with AI-generated commentary and regulatory interpretation

Case Study: "EisnerAmper GCC Pune – Automating Audit Analytics with Generative AI"

Conducted: Session 7, Hour 2 (in-class, 45 minutes discussion + 30 minutes written response)

Submitted to Academic Coordinator: Case material distributed and submitted to coordinator at least 2 sessions before the session


Case Summary: EisnerAmper's Global Capability Centre in Pune adopted generative AI tools to automate document review, extract financial data from unstructured PDFs, and draft preliminary audit findings. The case explores: technology stack selection, change management challenges, regulatory risk (PCAOB/ICAI audit standards), ROI measurement, and competitive implications for mid-tier audit firms entering the GCC model.


Discussion Questions:

  • What specific audit tasks were automated, and which AI techniques were applied to each?
  • Analyse the governance and data privacy risks EisnerAmper faced in deploying AI in audit workflows.
  • Using the AI governance framework from Session 7, evaluate EisnerAmper's approach. What controls were missing?
  • How does this GCC model create competitive moats, and what does it mean for entry-level finance careers?

Written Submission: Individual 1-page analytical response (submitted within 48 hours of class)
